## What general dentistry covers
General dentistry is the starting point for checking your teeth, gums and mouth and discussing changes you have noticed. It covers routine examinations, advice on preventing decay and treatment such as fillings when appropriate. A check-up also gives you a chance to ask about an old restoration, changes in your bite or an area that is difficult to keep clean. If you are looking for a general dentist in Sekkalai, you can start with a consultation at our Karaikudi clinic.
## When to make an appointment
Arrange a visit if you have toothache, a broken filling, new sensitivity or a tooth that chips while you eat. Bleeding gums, frequent food trapping or a sore place in the mouth are also worth discussing. Decay and gum disease may start without obvious symptoms, so a routine visit can still matter when you feel well. Tell the dentist if the discomfort wakes you up, appears only with cold drinks or lingers when you bite. These details help guide the examination.
## What happens at a consultation
The dentist asks about your dental and general health, medications and previous treatment. They examine your teeth and gums and may recommend an X-ray when it would help clarify what is happening between teeth or beneath an existing filling. The findings guide the next step. Early changes may call for preventive care and monitoring. A cavity may need a filling, a deeper infection may call for root canal treatment, and inflammation around several teeth may need its own gum assessment. Ask which findings require attention first and what can be checked at a later visit.
## After your visit
The next steps depend on what happened at the visit. If a filling was placed with local anaesthetic, follow the dentist’s advice about eating until the numbness wears off. Tell the clinic if your bite feels uneven or pain is getting worse. For prevention, ask for brushing and between-teeth cleaning advice suited to the areas that are difficult to reach. A dentist can recommend a review interval based on your risk of decay and gum disease, past restorations and current symptoms.
## Plan a first visit
Write down a short list of priorities before you arrive. You might want to address one sore tooth first and discuss a longer-term check-up separately. Tell the dentist if you have a recent medical procedure or a change in health. If a treatment is suggested, ask what would happen if you waited, what alternatives exist and how you can tell whether a follow-up is needed.
## Questions patients ask
? Should I book if I have no pain? | Yes, a check-up can identify early changes before they hurt. The dentist will suggest how often to return after assessing your teeth and gums. If a new symptom occurs between appointments, contact the clinic rather than waiting for your routine visit.
? Is an X-ray needed at every check-up? | The dentist decides based on your history, symptoms and examination. An X-ray can help answer a specific question, such as whether decay may be present between teeth. Bring recent images or reports if you already have them.
? Can treatment happen during the first visit? | The first visit is an opportunity to understand your concern and available options. A dentist may recommend another appointment if the assessment, imaging or treatment needs more time. If you have work, travel or school commitments, mention them when you book.
? What should I bring to a dental check-up? | Bring a list of current medications and allergies, relevant dental records and any questions you want to discuss. If you are coming for pain, note when it started, which side it affects and what makes it better or worse.
? What if I feel nervous about treatment? | Tell the team when you book and again before the examination. It can help to agree on a pause signal and ask the dentist to explain each step. Share what made a previous visit difficult so the team can plan the conversation around it.

## What a children’s dentist checks
Pedodontics is dentistry for infants, children and teenagers. Early appointments focus on how the teeth and gums are developing and on the habits that help protect them. The dentist may check for decay, discuss brushing with a parent or caregiver and ask about food and drink routines. At later visits, they can look at how baby teeth are being replaced and whether the growing bite needs attention. Families looking for children’s dentistry in Sekkalai can request an appointment at our Karaikudi clinic.
## When to bring your child
The American Dental Association advises a first dental visit after the first tooth appears and by the first birthday. If your child is older and has never been seen, book when you can. Make an appointment sooner if you notice a sore tooth, pain with eating, gum swelling, a damaged tooth, or an area that looks white, brown or dark and concerns you. After a fall or hit to the mouth, tell the clinic when it happened and whether a tooth moved, broke or came out.
## What to expect at the visit
The dentist will ask about your child’s health, medicines, past visits and any particular worries. For a very young child, an examination may be possible with the child on a caregiver’s lap. Depending on age, symptoms and findings, the dentist may discuss brushing technique, a cleaning, fluoride, sealants or a restoration. The dentist can explain whether a suggested measure protects a healthy tooth or treats a problem already present. If your child becomes upset, ask about taking a break or finishing the assessment at a separate visit.
## Preparing and following up
Use simple words before the visit, such as saying that the dentist will count and check the teeth. Tell the clinic about communication preferences, sensory sensitivities, allergies or anything that helped at an earlier appointment. Bring any records you have and allow time for questions. After treatment, follow the dentist’s instructions about eating and cleaning, especially while the cheek or lip is numb. For routine follow-up, ask how often to return and which tooth or habit needs particular attention at home.
## First appointments and follow-up
If your child recently had a dental injury, describe any change in colour or discomfort since it happened. If brushing has become difficult, tell the dentist which part of the routine causes trouble. A demonstration during the visit can give you something specific to practise at home. Ask whether you should return to check one tooth, have preventive care or continue with a routine assessment.
## Questions parents ask
? Why do baby teeth matter if they fall out? | Children use them to eat, speak and learn daily brushing. Decay or injury can cause pain before the tooth is ready to fall out. A dentist can assess whether the tooth needs treatment and explain what to watch for as it changes.
? Are X-rays part of every visit? | They are recommended when they would help answer a dental question. The dentist considers your child’s age, symptoms and risk of decay. Ask what the image would show and whether any recent images or records are useful.
? What if my child is scared? | Mention the concern when booking. You can describe what usually helps your child settle and ask how the exam can be introduced gradually. A parent or caregiver can ask for a short break if the child needs time to get comfortable.
? What is a dental sealant? | A sealant covers the grooves on a chewing surface where food and plaque can collect. The dentist checks which teeth have come through and whether a sealant would be useful for your child.
? How can I prepare a medication history? | Write down prescribed and non-prescribed medicines, any allergies and relevant health conditions. If something has changed since the last visit or another clinician has given specific instructions, tell the dentist before any examination or treatment.

## What dental scaling does
Scaling removes hardened plaque, known as tartar, that forms on the teeth and can collect near the gumline. Brushing and cleaning between the teeth remove soft plaque, while hardened deposits need professional removal. A routine cleaning and deep cleaning address different findings. When there are deeper gum pockets, a dentist may recommend cleaning below the gumline as part of periodontal treatment. If you are planning a dental cleaning in Sekkalai, the first step at our Karaikudi clinic is to ask for an assessment.
## Who may benefit from an assessment
Consider a visit if you see a rough deposit near the gumline, experience bleeding when brushing or have noticed persistent bad breath. A dentist can also look at areas that are hard to clean around bridges, crowded teeth or braces. Bleeding and tenderness can have different causes, so describe what happens and how long it has been going on. Let the dentist know if your teeth are already sensitive, if you have had previous gum treatment or if taking a medicine affects bleeding.
## What the appointment may involve
The dentist checks the gums and may measure the spaces between the teeth and gums. They explain where deposits have collected and which areas can be reached during routine scaling. Instruments are used to remove deposits, and polishing may be discussed for selected surface stains. If the examination suggests gum disease below the gumline, the dentist can explain whether deep cleaning, anaesthetic or a separate review is appropriate. Ask which areas of your mouth need special attention and how the findings will be checked over time.
## Aftercare and home cleaning
Follow any instructions specific to the cleaning you received. Gums may feel tender and teeth more sensitive after cleaning, especially when treatment reaches deeper pockets. If sensitivity persists or pain or bleeding increases, contact the clinic. Ask the dentist to show you how to clean between your teeth and what size of brush or other tool fits your spaces. A professional cleaning removes existing deposits. What you do between visits helps manage the plaque that builds up again.
## Ask about sensitive teeth and restorations
Let the dentist know if you avoid cold water, if an area feels sore when you brush or if you have an appliance that is awkward to clean. The dentist can plan how to examine those areas and explain how to care for restorations or implants. If routine cleaning uncovers inflammation or a site that bleeds easily, ask whether a periodontal review is recommended.
## Questions patients ask
? Can I remove tartar by brushing harder? | Firmly attached deposits usually require professional instruments. Brushing harder can irritate the gums. Ask the dentist to show you a gentler method for removing soft plaque and a way to clean spots that are easy to miss.
? Is scaling the same as whitening? | Scaling removes deposits, and polishing may help with some surface stains. Whitening changes the shade of natural teeth through a different process. An examination helps clarify whether the change you see is tartar, staining or something else.
? Will I always need deep cleaning? | The dentist may recommend deep cleaning if there are signs of gum disease and deposits beneath the gums. The depth of gum pockets and other findings guide this decision. Many visits involve routine cleaning only.
? How often should I get a cleaning? | The interval depends on how quickly deposits return, your gum health and previous treatment. Ask how your dentist arrived at the recommended interval and what changes would mean you should make an earlier appointment.
? Can I clean between teeth with braces? | Yes, ask your treating team to demonstrate how to reach around wires and brackets. There are tools made for different spaces. Take your current brush or cleaning tool to the appointment if you want help with technique.

## What periodontal care covers
Periodontal care deals with the gums and the bone that support the teeth. Inflammation can start around the gumline. In more advanced gum disease, the tissue and bone that hold teeth in place may be affected. A gum assessment can help distinguish early inflammation from a problem that needs more involved care and monitoring. For patients in Sekkalai, our Karaikudi clinic offers periodontal consultations. Bring any earlier gum measurements or X-rays if you have them.
## Signs worth discussing with a dentist
Tell the dentist if your gums bleed repeatedly, feel swollen or appear to be pulling away from your teeth. Persistent bad breath, shifting teeth or teeth that feel loose also deserve attention. Tell the team if you smoke, have diabetes, take medication that affects bleeding or have had gum treatment in the past. These details provide context for the examination. A recurring symptom can be worth checking even when pain is mild or absent.
## How a periodontal visit works
The dentist examines the gums and may use a small instrument to measure the spaces around individual teeth. X-rays may be recommended to check the bone supporting the teeth. Once you understand the findings, ask which sites need attention and what the treatment is meant to achieve. Options can include advice on home cleaning, professional cleaning below the gumline, or a specialist assessment if the problem is more advanced. You can ask how the team will check changes in bleeding or pocket depth at a later visit.
## What to expect after treatment
Mild tenderness and sensitivity can occur after deep cleaning. Follow the advice you receive for your teeth and gums and find out when the dentist plans to recheck the treated areas. If your symptoms are getting worse or a tooth becomes looser, contact the clinic. Daily cleaning between the teeth supports the work done at the visit. Ask how to clean around areas with recession or gaps and whether you should use a different tool around implants or bridgework.
## Prepare for a gum review
If you have been asked to return after treatment, bring along any questions about bleeding, sensitivity and the cleaning tools you were shown. It can help to describe exactly where a brush or floss catches. A change in pocket depth is only one piece of information. Ask how the measured sites, the visible tissue and your symptoms together affect the next step.
## Questions patients ask
? Do bleeding gums mean I have periodontitis? | Bleeding may be related to early inflammation or other causes. A dentist needs to look at gum pockets, tissue and sometimes X-rays before determining what is happening. Note how often you see bleeding and whether any areas are sore.
? Why are gum pockets measured again? | Measurements give the dentist a way to track particular sites after treatment. Changes in bleeding and pocket depth help guide maintenance or additional treatment. Ask the dentist to show which sites to focus on at home.
? Are routine cleaning and periodontal treatment different? | Routine scaling removes deposits around the visible tooth and gumline. Periodontal treatment can involve cleaning deeper around a tooth and checking how the gums respond. Your examination determines which approach is appropriate.
? Should I mention that I have diabetes? | Yes. Let the dental team know about your medical history and current medication. Ask whether any instructions from the clinician who manages your diabetes should be taken into account when planning your dental visit.
? Can gum problems affect dental implant planning? | Gum health is one part of assessing whether an implant is appropriate and how it can be maintained. If you have a missing tooth and a history of gum problems, ask how both issues would be managed in sequence.

## What root canal treatment treats
A root canal treats infection inside a tooth. The inside contains soft tissue called the pulp, and it can be affected by deep decay, a crack or injury. The dentist checks whether the tooth can be restored and whether retaining it is a suitable option. Root canal treatment aims to address infection while keeping the tooth when possible. If you are looking for a root canal assessment in Sekkalai, our Karaikudi clinic can examine the tooth and explain the findings.
## Who may need an assessment
Call about pain that lingers after hot or cold drinks, pain on biting, swelling near a tooth, or discomfort after a tooth has cracked. Symptoms alone do not identify the cause. A dentist may compare the affected tooth with nearby teeth, examine any existing filling and take an X-ray to assess the roots and surrounding bone. Tell the dentist when the pain started and whether it has changed. If you have rapidly spreading swelling or trouble swallowing or breathing, seek urgent care.
## How the procedure is planned
If the tooth is suitable for treatment, the dentist typically uses local anaesthetic before opening the tooth to reach the pulp. The canals are cleaned, shaped and sealed. A temporary filling may protect the tooth between visits. The dentist may recommend a permanent filling or crown once the canal work is complete, depending on where the tooth sits and how much structure remains. Ask how the tooth will be protected in the meantime and whether any older restoration needs to be removed or replaced.
## Recovery and review
Some soreness around the treated tooth can happen. Follow any instructions given to you about eating, brushing, medication and returning for the final restoration. If the dentist fits a temporary filling, ask which foods and chewing habits to avoid until the treatment is complete. Contact the clinic if swelling or pain worsens or a temporary filling comes out. The team can tell you what to expect at follow-up and explain why a restored tooth still needs regular care.
## Plan for two types of follow-up
Ask which appointment is for cleaning or sealing the roots and which is for restoring the outside of the tooth. Keeping those steps clear helps you plan travel and work. Tell the dentist if another clinician placed a temporary filling or started treatment already. Bring any notes or pictures from that visit so the team can assess where to continue.
## Questions patients ask
? Is a root canal always finished in one visit? | The number of appointments depends on the tooth, the infection and the restoration it needs. Some teeth require more than one visit. Ask which steps are planned for the first visit and how the tooth will be protected between appointments.
? Can it hurt afterwards? | Temporary soreness is possible. Local anaesthetic is usually used during the procedure. If you have an unusual reaction to anaesthetic, difficulty getting numb during past treatment or a particular concern about pain, tell the dentist before the procedure.
? Will I need a crown? | The dentist considers the position of the tooth, bite and remaining tooth structure. Some teeth need added protection from a crown. Ask how the proposed final restoration will protect your tooth and when you can have it placed.
? What if the tooth cannot be restored? | Ask the dentist to explain what makes the tooth difficult to retain. An extraction may be discussed. If a tooth is removed, the dentist can explain whether replacing it is appropriate and what options may suit your mouth.
? Can I wait if the pain has settled? | An improvement in pain does not identify the cause or show that an infection has cleared. Ask a dentist to assess the tooth, especially after swelling or a fracture, and describe earlier symptoms even if you feel comfortable now.

## What minor oral surgery covers
Minor oral surgery includes procedures such as removing a tooth that cannot be retained or assessing a wisdom tooth that is causing problems. A tooth may be difficult to remove if it is broken below the gumline or partly covered by gum or bone. An assessment helps clarify what can be done and whether referral is appropriate. Patients looking for oral surgery in Sekkalai can discuss their symptoms and dental history with the team at our Karaikudi clinic.
## When a consultation is appropriate
Tell the dentist about repeated pain or swelling around a wisdom tooth, difficulty cleaning the back of your mouth, or prior episodes of infection. If another dentist has advised removal, bring any referral and recent X-rays. Share health conditions, previous reactions to anaesthesia and all current medicines, especially if you take a medicine that affects bleeding. Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own. Tell the team if you have a trip or other commitment soon after the proposed visit.
## How removal may be planned
The dentist may examine the tooth and recommend an X-ray to see its position and roots. Some teeth can be removed without opening the gum. Others may require a small opening, removal in sections or stitches. Local anaesthetic is commonly used. The dentist can discuss how they manage comfort and whether a referral is appropriate based on the tooth and your health. Ask what they expect to do, why they recommend it and what follow-up they plan.
## Recovery and aftercare
Follow the instructions you receive about looking after the socket, eating, brushing and what to do if it bleeds. Protecting the clot matters during early healing. Some tenderness and swelling can occur, and a dentist can explain the expected course for your procedure. Seek advice if pain or swelling gets worse, you develop a fever or bleeding does not settle. Ask who to contact outside clinic hours and whether your stitches or the healing site need checking.
## Before agreeing to a procedure
Ask whether removal is the only option or whether the tooth can be assessed for treatment first. Ask what information the X-ray provides about nearby teeth and other structures. Before leaving, make sure you know which instructions apply for the day of surgery and which apply from the following day. Check who to contact if something feels unusual.
## Questions patients ask
? Does every wisdom tooth need removal? | A wisdom tooth that causes no problems can often be monitored. Removal may be considered if it repeatedly becomes inflamed, has decay or is affecting a nearby tooth. The examination and any necessary imaging help the dentist explain your options.
? Can I return to work the same day? | This depends on the procedure, the type of anaesthesia and your work. Ask about time off and travel before you book. If sedation is planned, follow the instructions about arranging a companion and avoiding driving afterwards.
? What if I take a blood thinner? | Bring its name and dose and say why you take it. Your dentist can plan around your medical history and communicate with the prescribing clinician if needed. Keep taking it as prescribed unless your treating clinicians advise a change.
? Will the gap need a replacement tooth? | It depends on which tooth is being removed, your bite and the remaining teeth. Replacing a functional tooth may be discussed. Ask whether an implant, bridge or another option is relevant once healing has been assessed.
? What is dry socket? | After an extraction, a clot usually protects the healing socket. A problem with the clot can cause worsening pain. If pain increases after initially improving, contact the treating dentist so they can examine the socket and recommend care.

## What a dental implant replaces
A dental implant is a post placed in the jaw to support a replacement tooth. It can hold a crown or help support a bridge or denture, depending on how many teeth are missing and the condition of your mouth. The dentist can compare implants with other ways to replace missing teeth and explain what can be expected from each option. If you live in Sekkalai or elsewhere in Karaikudi, you can request an implant consultation at the clinic.
## Who may be suitable
A dentist assesses where a tooth is missing, the amount of supporting bone, gum health and how your upper and lower teeth meet. Smoking, some health conditions and certain medicines can affect healing. Tell your dentist about any history of gum problems, previous surgery or medical treatment that may affect your bones. If you already use a partial denture or have bridgework, explain how it feels when you eat and clean around it. That information can help the dentist compare different options with you.
## How the treatment is staged
The first stage is an examination and imaging as needed. The dentist explains where an implant might go and whether another problem must be addressed first. If you choose the procedure and it is appropriate, the implant is placed in the jaw. The bone heals around it over time. The dentist checks when it can support the planned crown or other restoration. Some people use a temporary tooth during part of this period. Ask what will be possible at each stage and how the provisional tooth would be maintained.
## Recovery and long-term care
Follow instructions for eating, cleaning and returning for checks after surgery. Call the clinic if discomfort or swelling worsens or the site bleeds persistently. Once the final replacement is fitted, the surrounding gum still needs regular cleaning and review. Ask how to clean the spaces around the replacement and what changes, including bleeding or movement, should prompt a visit. How long the treatment takes and how it functions depend on the individual plan, healing and maintenance.
## Bring your priorities to the consultation
Tell the dentist whether chewing, appearance, speech or keeping an existing denture stable is your main concern. That can change which alternatives are worth comparing. Consider asking the team to outline the order of visits, what might cause a delay and what would happen if healing takes longer than expected. Bring details of earlier dental work if they are available.
## Questions patients ask
? Is an implant suitable for everyone with a missing tooth? | Suitability depends on your gums, jawbone, health and the location of the gap. A dentist can outline which factors affect your options and whether you should consider a bridge or a denture.
? Is an implant the same as a crown? | The implant is the part that sits in the jaw. The crown is the visible replacement tooth it can support. They are planned together, and the dentist checks that there is room and support for both.
? How long will I wait for the final tooth? | Healing varies between people and sites. Some plans have several stages. Ask when the dentist will recheck healing and whether a temporary replacement is suitable if the gap affects speaking, eating or appearance.
? Can several missing teeth be replaced? | Depending on your mouth, more than one tooth may be replaced using individual crowns, a bridge or a denture supported by implants. Ask which areas are suitable for support and how you would clean around the proposed restoration.
? What if I have had gum disease? | Mention previous gum care before planning an implant. The dentist may recommend addressing gum inflammation first and monitoring it throughout care. Ask what your maintenance visits would include and what you can do at home.

## What orthodontic treatment can address
Braces and aligners move teeth over time. A consultation can explore whether treatment would help with crowding, spacing or a bite concern. Fixed braces use brackets and a wire. Children, teenagers and adults can have questions about the position of their teeth. Bring up spaces that are hard to keep clean, teeth that do not meet comfortably or concerns about a growing bite. The clinician checks tooth position, the bite and the condition of the mouth. They may take photos, scans or other records as appropriate. Temporary soreness can happen when an appliance is new or has been adjusted. ? How long does treatment take? | The timeline depends on tooth movement, the appliance and how the teeth respond. The clinician can estimate it after assessing you. Keeping appointments and following instructions may help the team identify issues before they delay progress.
? Why do I need a retainer? | Teeth can move after braces or aligners are finished. A retainer helps keep them in their new position. Ask how to clean yours and how to recognise when it has stopped fitting properly.

## Questions patients ask
? Will whitening change the colour of a crown? | Whitening affects natural teeth. A crown or filling may stay the same shade, making an existing difference more noticeable. Before treatment, ask the dentist to show which surfaces are restored and what that means for your options.
? Is a cleaning the same as whitening? | A cleaning removes deposits and can address some surface stains. Whitening changes the shade of natural teeth using a different process. Which approach is relevant depends on the reason for the colour you see.
? What if my teeth already feel sensitive? | Tell the dentist when it happens and what triggers it. They can examine the cause and explain whether whitening should be postponed or whether another option would be more appropriate.
? Are veneers the only option for tooth shape? | Options depend on the tooth and how it functions. Ask whether monitoring, smoothing or a restoration is appropriate. If the recommendation would involve removing enamel, ask how much would be needed and what the alternatives involve.
? What should I ask before deciding? | Ask what problem the treatment is addressing, how the dentist would measure success and what maintenance or side effects are possible. If you have an upcoming event, ask about timelines without assuming the treatment can be rushed.
