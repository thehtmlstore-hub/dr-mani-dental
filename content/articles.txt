# Root canal in Karaikudi: what to expect
@ root-canal-what-to-expect
! How to prepare for a root canal consultation, what treatment involves and what to ask about the final restoration.
> NHS root canal treatment | https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/root-canal-treatment/
+ root-canal
## Before the appointment
Note when the pain began, what triggers it and whether it wakes you up. Bring your medication list and any previous X-rays. A dentist may examine the tooth and nearby teeth and recommend imaging to assess the roots. Root canal treatment may be advised if the soft tissue inside the tooth is infected and the tooth can be restored.
## From treatment to a final restoration
The dentist typically numbs the area, cleans inside the tooth and seals the canals. Ask whether more than one visit may be needed and whether you will have a temporary filling. A permanent filling or crown can protect the tooth afterwards. Follow your instructions about chewing and return for the restoration at the time advised. If swelling or pain increases, contact the treating team.

# Dental implants vs bridges
@ dental-implants-vs-bridges
! Compare how implants and bridges support replacement teeth, what treatment involves and how you would clean them.
> ADA MouthHealthy implants | https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/implants
> ADA MouthHealthy bridges | https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/bridges
+ dental-implants
## What supports the new tooth?
A dental implant is supported by a post placed in the jaw. A traditional fixed bridge can use nearby teeth to support an artificial tooth across the gap. The health of the supporting teeth, the gum and bone around the gap and your health history can affect the choice. Some gaps can also be managed with a removable replacement.
## Compare treatment and maintenance
Ask how each option would affect adjacent teeth, how many stages are likely and whether you might use a temporary tooth. An implant requires surgery and healing. A bridge supported by teeth may require work on those teeth. Both need ongoing cleaning. Ask the dentist to show how you would clean beneath a bridge or around an implant crown and how they would check these areas at follow-up.

# A child’s first dental visit
@ first-dental-visit-for-children
! A short guide for parents on when to book, how to prepare and what a dentist may check at the first visit.
> ADA MouthHealthy first dental visit | https://www.mouthhealthy.org/life-stages/babies-and-kids/first-dental-visit-for-baby
+ pedodontics
## When to book and what to bring
The American Dental Association advises taking a child to the dentist after the first tooth appears and by the first birthday. If your child is older, you can still start with a check-up. Bring relevant health information, allergies and anything you notice during eating or brushing. Mention any communication or sensory needs when you make the appointment.
## What the dentist looks at
The dentist checks the teeth, gums and developing bite. A young child may be examined while sitting on a caregiver’s lap. Ask for a demonstration of brushing and advice on foods and drinks, fluoride and when to return. If your child needs a break, tell the team. Simple words like “count and check your teeth” can help explain the visit before you arrive.

# Braces vs clear aligners
@ braces-vs-clear-aligners
! Understand the differences between fixed braces and removable aligners and what daily care each option involves.
> NHS braces guide | https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/braces/
+ braces-aligners
## What you wear
Fixed braces use brackets and a wire to guide tooth movement. Clear aligners are fitted removable trays that are changed in a planned sequence. The movements needed for your bite and the condition of your gums and teeth help determine which options are suitable. Ask what the clinician is trying to change before comparing appliances.
## What changes day to day
Fixed braces stay on your teeth during meals and need careful cleaning around the brackets. Aligners are generally removed for meals and need consistent wear as prescribed. Both approaches need monitoring. Ask what to do if something breaks or stops fitting and how the clinician will measure progress. After active treatment, a retainer helps to hold the teeth in position.

# Wisdom tooth removal recovery
@ wisdom-tooth-removal-recovery
! What to discuss before wisdom tooth removal and when to call the treating team during recovery.
> NHS wisdom tooth removal | https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/wisdom-tooth-removal/
+ minor-oral-surgeries
## Plan for getting home and resting
Ask the treating team what type of anaesthetic is planned and whether you need someone to collect you. Mention medicines and medical conditions before the appointment. Ask how to manage eating, cleaning and activity during the early days of healing. Use the instructions you receive for your own procedure.
## Watch how symptoms change
Some soreness and swelling may follow an extraction. Ask what recovery should look like and which changes need attention. Contact the treating team if pain or swelling becomes more severe, bleeding does not settle, or you develop a fever. Know how to reach care outside clinic hours. Seek emergency care if you have swelling that affects breathing or swallowing.

# Why gums bleed
@ bleeding-gums-causes
! Understand common reasons gums bleed and what a dentist may ask or check if the bleeding keeps happening.
> ADA MouthHealthy bleeding gums | https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/bleeding-gums
> NHS gum disease | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gum-disease/
+ periodontal,scaling
## Look for a pattern
Gums may bleed because of inflammation, a change in brushing or flossing technique, or other factors. Tell the dentist whether the bleeding occurs in one place or several, how often it happens and whether you also see swelling or recession. Share your medications and any recent changes in health.
## What an assessment can show
The dentist looks for plaque, tartar and inflammation and may measure the spaces around the teeth. The findings help distinguish a problem that responds to better cleaning from a condition that needs periodontal treatment. Ask for a demonstration of how to clean between your teeth. Arrange a check if bleeding recurs, and seek prompt advice if bleeding is heavy or does not stop.

# Teeth whitening safety
@ teeth-whitening-safety
! Learn why a dental check matters before whitening and how natural teeth and restorations can respond differently.
> NHS teeth whitening | https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/teeth-whitening/
+ cosmetic-dentistry
## Start with a dental check
A dentist can look for decay, gum inflammation and causes of a colour change before you consider whitening. Tell them about sensitivity, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and previous whitening. A dentist can also identify fillings, crowns or veneers that will not lighten in the same way as natural teeth.
## Discuss side effects and follow-up
Whitening may cause temporary sensitivity or gum irritation. Ask how the dentist will protect the gums and how to use any product they recommend. Report side effects that concern you. Ask which changes you can reasonably expect, especially if the tooth colour is uneven, and how any result would be maintained.

# Scaling and gum health
@ scaling-and-gum-health
! Understand why tartar needs professional removal and how daily plaque removal supports gum health between visits.
> ADA MouthHealthy scaling and root planing | https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/scaling-and-root-planing/
> NHS gum disease | https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gum-disease/
+ scaling,periodontal
## Plaque and tartar are different
Plaque is a soft film that gathers on teeth. Regular brushing and between-teeth cleaning help remove it. Once deposits harden into tartar, professional instruments are usually needed. A dentist can identify areas where deposits recur and explain whether routine scaling or periodontal care is appropriate.
## After the cleaning
Ask for advice on reaching the areas that collected deposits, such as behind crowded teeth or around a bridge. Deep cleaning may be followed by a check of the gums and pocket measurements. If tenderness, sensitivity or bleeding continues beyond the advice you received, contact the clinic. Daily care and follow-up can help you address changes before they become harder to manage.
